SKY SENTINEL

Know what's watching. A pocket detector for license-plate cameras, body cams, trackers, camera glasses, drones and card skimmers.

Runs on $10 to $40 ESP32 boards. Listen only: it never transmits to the devices it finds. Free for personal use, source included. No account, no cloud, no app.

What it finds

Every board listens to Wi-Fi and Bluetooth (and, with the right board, LoRa) and matches what it hears against signatures built from public research and the official IEEE and Bluetooth registries. The full list.

ALPR cameras

Flock Safety cameras and Raven gunshot sensors: their Wi-Fi names, Bluetooth IDs and Flock's own registered addresses.

Body cams

Axon / TASER, Motorola Solutions and WatchGuard, Digital Ally, Utility BodyWorn, Zepcam.

Trackers

AirTags and Find My tags away from their owner, SmartTags, Tile, Chipolo, Google Find Hub tags. Alerts when one follows you.

Camera glasses

Ray-Ban and Oakley Meta, Snap Spectacles, Vuzix.

Drones

FAA Remote ID decoded: the drone's serial number, where it is, its height, speed and heading, and where the pilot is standing. Plus DJI DroneID, DJI / Parrot / Skydio radios and drone hotspots.

Card skimmers

The cheap serial Bluetooth radios (HC-05, HC-06 and friends) found hidden in gas pumps.

Attacks and hacker gear

Evil-twin access points (Wi-Fi Pineapple style), Wi-Fi beacon spam, deauth floods, Bluetooth spam bursts, Flipper Zero, Pwnagotchi. Fakes are kept out of the counts.

Following and lingering

A device that stays with you across places, or keeps hanging around while you're parked, raises its own alert.

Vehicles

Teslas, car makers' Wi-Fi hotspots, car stereos and OBD-II dongles. In a moving car, the same vehicle staying behind you raises a following alert.

Phones and everything around

Phones, tablets and hotspots, even ones already on Wi-Fi; earbuds with battery levels; routers with security, Wi-Fi generation, load and uptime; which network each device is on; Windows PCs and Xbox; smart-home gear in setup mode.

LoRa and Meshtastic

Meshtastic nodes with their names, positions and battery from the public channel; LoRaWAN trackers and sensors by their device IDs.

A log with time and place

Every find is saved with GPS time and location, in the master's flash and on a CYD's SD card. Opens in Excel or Google My Maps.

One board, or a cluster

A single ESP32-C5 is a complete detector. Add more boards and they pair to it as nodes over an encrypted ESP-NOW link: the master shares out the work (which radios, which channels), merges what everyone hears, and runs the console. Each kind of board adds something the others can't hear.

Supported boards

What each board adds to the cluster. Outlined in blue: something only that kind of board brings. Classic Bluetooth needs an original ESP32 (the CYD or a WROOM DevKit); the C5 and S3 chips don't have it.

Get started in three steps

1. Install

Plug a board into a desktop running Chrome, Edge or Firefox 151+ and press Install. No software to download.

2. Open the console

The console connects over USB. Or join the master's own Wi-Fi from your phone and it opens by itself.

3. Add nodes

Press PAIR A NODE, hold the new board close, check the 6-digit code matches, ACCEPT. How pairing works.

SkySentinel listens. It records radio signals that devices broadcast publicly and never jams or attacks anything. It only talks to a device if you choose to (the optional ASK button and Bluetooth "ask" scanning, both off by default). Check the laws where you use it, and use what it finds responsibly.
