DEPLOYMENT
Where this board lives

Mobile (vehicle) and Portable (on foot) warn when a device stays with you as you move: a tracker after about 3 places and 10 to 15 minutes, anything else after 5 places and 20 to 30 minutes. Movement is judged from the Wi-Fi networks around you changing (GPS when a board has it). Stationary warns when surveillance gear lingers nearby for 20 minutes. Mark your own devices with THIS IS MINE in their details so they don't alert.

BLUETOOTH SCANNING
Ask devices for details

Listen only is silent: the board never transmits. Ask sends a short request to each device, and most answer with extra details, often their name ("Raycon E25", "Forerunner 265"). Devices can tell they were asked.

Open a device's details: ASK it right away (this browser)

Same as pressing ASK in the details window, only for the device you open, and only when it accepts connections. It connects for a few seconds (the device can see that) and pauses scanning meanwhile.

WI-FI NETWORKS PHONES LOOK FOR
Show them in a phone's details

Phones call out by name for networks they've joined before ("Starbucks WiFi", "Hilton Honors", a home network), so the list can show where someone has been. Off by default. The master keeps them in memory only, never in the log or on the SD card, and turning this off forgets them.

DETECTION LOG

Every surveillance device found, following and lingering alerts, floods and spam, with the time and place when a GPS node (the Heltec) has a fix, plus a track point every minute while moving. Kept in this board's flash (the newest several thousand records survive power cuts) and written to the SD card of every CYD in the cluster. Opens in Excel or Google Sheets; the lat / lon columns import into Google My Maps.

MAINTENANCE

HOW SKYSENTINEL WORKS

SkySentinel listens to the radio around you and shows the devices it hears: surveillance gear (ALPR plate cameras, body cams, trackers, drones, skimmers, pentest tools) and everyday devices (phones, earbuds, watches, access points). It only listens unless you tell it to ask. Nothing leaves the board except to this page.

Use this page offline

This whole page is one file. Save it and open it later with no internet: double-click it, then CONNECT USB. (Chrome, Edge or Firefox 151+.) A master with its access point on also serves it to your phone.

A saved copy doesn't update itself: save it again after you update the firmware, so the page and the boards match. Flashing firmware still needs the website.

No board hears everything at once: the C5 has one radio that takes turns between Wi-Fi channels and Bluetooth. More boards = more of the air covered at the same time.

Standalone, master and node
  • Standalone: one board does everything. It hops all Wi-Fi channels and scans Bluetooth, keeps the device list, and feeds this page. Right for a single C5.
  • Master: the brain of a cluster. Pairs with nodes over ESP-NOW (a direct radio link, no router), tells each one what to scan, and merges what they hear into one list. It stays on Wi-Fi channel 6 to hear its nodes, and its own radio still listens there and to Bluetooth.
  • Node: a sensor. Scans what the master assigns and reports back once a second. Connect this page to the master, not a node, to see devices.

The master splits the work by what each node can do. Example: a C5 node takes 5 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth LE, an original-ESP32 node takes 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Classic Bluetooth. Mirror mode gives every node the same plan instead, so the same device is heard by all of them and signal strengths can be compared for direction. Change the role any time in SETTINGS; no reflashing.

Nodes join a master by pairing (below), so strangers' boards can't join yours and yours won't join theirs.

Pairing a node
Reading the screen
  • Radar: distance from the centre is signal strength (strong = near the middle). One board can't tell direction, so the angle only spreads the dots out. Filled dots are surveillance categories, rings are everything else, bigger = more confident.
  • NOW / SESSION / ALERTS: devices heard in the last minute, all devices since the board started, and alerts raised.
  • Category tiles: now / session per category. Click a tile to turn that category on or off; CATEGORIES changes several at once and shows the scan plan.
  • Device list: hover a column header for what it means. Red and amber rows are surveillance gear. Click any row for its details.
  • +N NETWORKS: one router broadcasting several networks (main, guest, IoT, 2.4 and 5 GHz). Click it for the SSIDs.
How devices are identified
  • Signatures for specific gear: Flock ALPR cameras and Raven gunshot sensors, body cams (Axon, Motorola, WatchGuard, Digital Ally), camera glasses (Ray-Ban / Oakley Meta, Snap Spectacles, Vuzix), drones (Remote ID, DJI DroneID, DJI, Parrot, Skydio), Apple Find My, Samsung SmartTag, Tile, Chipolo, Google trackers, drone Remote ID, Flipper Zero, Wi-Fi Pineapple, AirPods and Beats (with model), Fast Pair, Garmin, and more.
  • What the device says it is: many Bluetooth devices announce a type (earbuds, headphones, watch, keyboard) and a maker number. MAKER comes from the official Bluetooth company list.
  • Changing addresses: phones, earbuds and trackers change their address every few minutes for privacy. SkySentinel fingerprints the parts of their broadcast that don't change and links the new address to the old one (ADDRESS shows ↻ and a count).
  • BLE spam: tools like a Flipper flood the air with fake devices. A burst of fakeable adverts from one signal level raises an alert, and the fakes are kept out of the counts.
  • Deauth floods: Wi-Fi attack frames are counted per channel; a flood raises an alert.
  • Wi-Fi beacon spam: a burst of brand-new networks at one signal level (Flipper, Marauder) raises an alert; the fake networks are kept out of the counts and the list.
  • Evil twin: one radio answering as several different network names, the way a Wi-Fi Pineapple lures phones, raises an alert whatever address it uses.
  • Drones: Remote ID is decoded: serial number, position, height, speed and heading, and where the pilot is.
  • Vehicles: Teslas, car Wi-Fi hotspots, car stereos and OBD-II dongles.
Deployment and following alerts
  • Stationary (a fixed array, a desk): warns when surveillance gear lingers nearby for 20 minutes.
  • Mobile (in a vehicle) and Portable (on foot): warns when something stays with you as you move. A tracker (AirTag away from its owner, SmartTag, Tile, Chipolo, Google) alerts after about 3 places and 10 minutes driving, 15 walking. Anything else alerts after 5 places and 20 to 30 minutes, which catches an unknown device riding along.
  • How it knows you moved: about once a minute it looks at the Wi-Fi networks around you. When most of them are new, that's a new place (the header shows the place count). Boards with GPS will use distance instead. In open country with almost no Wi-Fi it can't tell, so it waits.
  • Your own gear follows you too. Open its details and press THIS IS MINE; it won't alert in this browser again (devices that change addresses only stay marked for this session).
Settings worth knowing
  • Categories: fewer categories = faster scanning. Body cams + trackers only means Bluetooth full time.
  • Bluetooth scanning, Listen only / Ask: listen only never transmits. Ask sends each device a short request, and most reply with more detail, often their name. Devices can tell they were asked.
  • Ask one device (in a device's details): connects to just that device for a few seconds and reads its name, maker, model, firmware and battery. Only for devices that accept connections.
  • Wi-Fi access point: the board makes its own network and serves this page, so any phone works with no cable or internet. An access point can't hop, so a standalone board's Wi-Fi scanning drops to channel 6 (free on a master).
  • Night: red theme for dark cars and dark-adapted eyes.
CYD touch dashboard

A CYD node shows what the master sees, without a phone or laptop:

  • Banner: the latest alert (red) or notice (amber), with how long ago. Tap it to clear. Alerts also blink the board's LED red and beep (turn the beep off in its settings).
  • Tiles: devices heard in the last 30 s per category. Tap a tile to switch that category on or off for the whole cluster, same as CATEGORIES here.
  • Closest: the five nearest surveillance devices, following ones first, with signal strength. Grey = not heard for 10 s.
  • Header: its name (set with EDIT in CLUSTER), deployment, nodes online, battery (ESP32-32E). Tap it for settings: deployment, beep, touch setup, display check, board, upside down.

Tabs along the bottom: DEVICES (the live list, tap a row for details), EVENTS (alert history, nodes joining and dropping out) and CLUSTER (every board's link, battery, GPS and health). SETTINGS has a red night theme with a dimmed backlight.

NODE SCREENS OFF darkens it too; a tap wakes it for 30 seconds. Updates arrive about once a second.

What it can't see
  • Classic Bluetooth on a C5: its radio is Bluetooth LE only. An original-ESP32 node finds Classic devices while they're discoverable (pairing mode, cars, many speakers), but not ones already paired and streaming, like headphones on a call. That takes a dedicated sniffer (Ubertooth).
  • Quiet devices: a watch or earbuds connected to a phone often stop advertising until they lose the phone.
  • Other bands: cellular, and GPS trackers that only talk over cellular. 915 MHz LoRa needs the Heltec node.
  • LoRa, one channel at a time: on AUTO the Heltec takes turns between Meshtastic LongFast, MediumFast, MeshCore and LoRaWAN, so it can miss packets sent while it listens elsewhere. Pin it to one (CLUSTER > EDIT > LORA) to hear everything there. It reads headers and public broadcasts only (who sent it, names and positions nodes announce, how many hops), never message contents. MeshCore nodes show up when they send an advert (repeaters every few hours, companions when the owner sends one).
  • Content: SkySentinel reads broadcasts and headers, never anyone's data.
Connecting
  • USB: Chrome or Edge on a desktop, CONNECT USB, pick the board's port. Only one program can use the port, so close serial monitors first.
  • Wi-Fi: turn on the access point in SETTINGS, join that network from a phone, and the page opens by itself (or go to http://192.168.4.1). Works in any browser, including Safari.
  • Installing firmware: FIRMWARE (USB only) installs or updates a board right from this page.

Full details, serial commands and build steps: README.md in the firmware folder.

FIRMWARE install or update over USB

1 · FIRMWARE FILE

From your build folder: .pio/build/c5/firmware.bin updates a C5 and keeps its settings; firmware.factory.bin is a fresh install that erases them. Use classic for the original ESP32, v8 for the Marauder.

How to flash each kind of board
2 · FLASH

Close anything else using the board's port first (serial monitor, PlatformIO). If this page is connected, it hands the port over. An original ESP32 that won't start flashing: hold its BOOT button while you press FLASH.

NAME

Shows on its card here and on the node's own screen.

JOB

AUTO lets the master share the work out. Pin a job when you know where a node is, e.g. a box by the road on Wi-Fi only, or one inside the truck on Bluetooth only. EVERYTHING is for a node somewhere else (another room, the far side of the house): it scans every channel and Bluetooth on its own, taking turns, so it can miss a little; the other nodes share the work between them as if it weren't there.

LINK TO THE MASTER

FOLLOW MASTER uses SETTINGS > Long Range. NORMAL is fastest, for nodes near the master. LR reaches several times further: 500 for most, 250 for the most reach. A node that loses the master still calls in both ways.

PAIR A NODE

Node not showing up?
  • Is it waiting to be paired? A new board, or one just updated from an older version, waits on its own. A board that belongs to another master has to be told first: hold its BOOT button for 5 seconds (C5 DevKit, Heltec, ESP32 DevKit), or on a CYD tap SETTINGS > Pair with a master twice. Its LED blinks blue slowly (or its screen says NOT PAIRED YET).
  • Closer. Only boards within about arm's reach of the master can pair, so a stranger's board down the street can't. Rows marked MOVE CLOSER are heard but too far away.
  • Both up to date? The master and the node need firmware from the same day or later (FIRMWARE, then UPDATE).
  • Still nothing? Plug the node into this computer, CONNECT USB to it: its page shows whether it's waiting and the code it sees.
